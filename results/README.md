All numbers are mean absolute error (MAE) in bikes; lower is better. The test
week is 23–30 October 2026 (266 stations, ~178,000 pairs per horizon). It was
used once, after every choice had been made on a validation week inside the
training period (16–23 October).

## Main result: LightGBM vs `unchanged`, test week

| Subset | 15 min | 30 min | 60 min |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| All hours | 0.224 vs 0.221 (−1 %) | 0.385 vs 0.394 (+2 %) | 0.632 vs 0.669 (+6 %) |
| Daytime (07–21) | 0.328 vs 0.325 (−1 %) | 0.556 vs 0.571 (+3 %) | 0.896 vs 0.956 (+6 %) |
| Weekday rush | 0.581 vs 0.588 (+1 %) | **0.918 vs 1.012 (+9 %)** | **1.393 vs 1.667 (+16 %)** |

LightGBM first, `unchanged` second, gain in brackets (positive = model better).
Docks, derived from the predicted bikes, show the same picture
(rush 60 min: 1.386 vs 1.667).

## What the model beats, and what it does not

- **It beats `unchanged` where it matters most: weekday rush at 30 and 60
  minutes (−9 % and −16 % error).** This is when stations empty or fill and a
  user most needs a forecast. The notebook `notebooks/02_model.ipynb` shows the
  gain is mostly in the *morning* rush (about a quarter less error at 07–09 at
  60 minutes); the afternoon rush improves much less.
- **It does not beat `unchanged` at 15 minutes.** Over all hours it is 1 %
  worse. In 15 minutes almost nothing changes, so predicting a change mostly
  adds error. A simple fix for the API is to show the current value at 15
  minutes; this was not tuned on the test week, so it is left as a choice for
  Phase 5.
- **The gain grows with the horizon** (−1 %, +2 %, +6 % overall), because the
  further ahead, the more the daily pattern matters compared with the current
  value.
- **What the model has learned:** which station behaves how at what time of day
  (station ID 43 % and time of day 19 % of the gain), plus the current number
  of bikes (17 %). Recent changes and last week's change add little.

## After the test week (November)

The same model, not retrained, on 30 October – 19 November (271 stations,
~514,000 pairs per horizon; this period was never used for any choice):

| Subset | 15 min | 30 min | 60 min |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| All hours | 0.173 vs 0.168 (−3 %) | 0.303 vs 0.301 (−1 %) | 0.504 vs 0.519 (+3 %) |
| Weekday rush | 0.427 vs 0.421 (−1 %) | 0.701 vs 0.740 (+5 %) | 1.067 vs 1.236 (+14 %) |

Errors are lower for both models in November because there are fewer trips in
the cold. The model still beats `unchanged` clearly in rush at 60 minutes, but
the gain has shrunk everywhere, and at 30 minutes over all hours it is now
slightly worse. It was trained on October only, so this is expected drift; it
should be retrained regularly.

## Experiments that did not help

- **Weather** (temperature and precipitation at Blindern, known one hour after
  the observation): slightly worse on the validation week. Temperature mostly
  acts as a calendar in a period where it falls steadily, and November is
  colder than anything in training. Left out of the model, code kept.
- **L2 and Huber loss** got worse from the first tree; MAE needs the median
  change, which is usually 0.

## Limitations

- **Short history:** 18 days of training, one test week. One unusual day can
  move the numbers, and the model has seen each weekday and time only 2–3
  times.
- **Redistribution trucks** can move 20–30 bikes at once. No feature can see
  them coming, and they are the largest remaining errors.
- **Only October** in training. The model has not seen winter, holidays or
  events, and the gain is already smaller in November.
- **No uncertainty:** the model gives one number. A user would also like to
  know how sure it is (for example "probably 0–2 bikes").
- **Stations that are inactive or frozen** were left out of training and
  evaluation. The API must handle them separately (`reliable = False`).

## Phase 3: baselines

`unchanged` (predict the current value) beat both other baselines almost
everywhere. "Same time last week" was far worse (MAE about 4.3) because the
*level* at a station differs a lot from week to week; the pattern lies in the
*change*. "Current value + mean change per station, weekday and time" won only
at 60 minutes in weekday rush (1.627 vs 1.667), since each cell had only 2–3
examples. That small gain suggested a model that pools examples, like gradient
boosting, could use the daily pattern, which Phase 4 confirmed. Docks derived
from the current total (bikes + docks) are as accurate as bikes, although
bikes + docks equals capacity in only 25.6 % of rows. Five stations were frozen
during part of the test week (430, 473, 625, 4552, 5424) and excluded.
