The test week is 23–30 October 2026 (266 stations, ~178,000 pairs per
horizon). Every choice was made on a validation week inside the training
period (16–23 October); the test week was only used to report the final
numbers.

## 1. Will there be a bike? (probability models)

This is the question a user asks, so it is the main result. Two classifiers
give P(at least one bike) and P(at least one free dock) 15, 30 and 60 minutes
ahead. Scored with the Brier score (mean squared difference between the
probability and what happened; lower is better) against two baselines:
`unchanged` (100 % if there is one now, else 0 %) and a count table (how often
there was at least one at the target in training, given the number now).

Brier score, test week, 15 / 30 / 60 min:

| Target | Subset | `unchanged` | count table | LightGBM | Gain vs table, 60 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bike | All hours | .019 / .034 / .054 | .018 / .030 / .045 | **.017 / .028 / .040** | +13 % |
| Bike | Weekday rush | .042 / .069 / .108 | .038 / .058 / .085 | **.034 / .049 / .068** | +20 % |
| Bike | ≤ 3 bikes now | .047 / .083 / .130 | .044 / .073 / .108 | **.042 / .067 / .094** | +13 % |
| Dock | Weekday rush | .016 / .026 / .040 | .014 / .021 / .031 | **.013 / .019 / .027** | +15 % |

- **The model beats both baselines at every horizon and subset**, most where it
  matters: weekday rush at 60 minutes, 20 % below the count table and 37 %
  below `unchanged` for bikes.
- **The probabilities are honest.** `calibration.png` shows that when the
  bike model says 30 %, it happens about 30 % of the time. The dock model is
  slightly overconfident in the middle (it says 45 %, it happens about 37 %).
- **The count table is a strong baseline**: it is calibrated by construction
  and knows that one bike now is risky. Beating it shows the model adds
  information beyond "how many now".

## 2. How many bikes? (MAE model)

Mean absolute error in bikes, LightGBM vs `unchanged` (gain in brackets,
positive = model better):

| Subset | 15 min | 30 min | 60 min |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| All hours | 0.224 vs 0.221 (−1 %) | 0.384 vs 0.394 (+2 %) | 0.627 vs 0.669 (+6 %) |
| Daytime (07–21) | 0.328 vs 0.325 (−1 %) | 0.554 vs 0.571 (+3 %) | 0.887 vs 0.956 (+7 %) |
| Weekday rush | 0.581 vs 0.588 (+1 %) | **0.914 vs 1.012 (+10 %)** | **1.360 vs 1.667 (+18 %)** |

Docks, derived from the predicted bikes, show the same picture (rush 60 min:
1.351 vs 1.667). The first Phase 4 model, without neighbour and city-wide
features, scored 1.393 (+16 %) at rush 60 min on the same test week; it is
kept in Git history (`results/model.md` at commit `71e4916`).

- **It beats `unchanged` where it matters: weekday rush at 30 and 60 minutes.**
  `notebooks/02_model.ipynb` shows the gain is mostly in the morning rush
  (about 27 % less error at 07–09 at 60 minutes); the afternoon improves less.
- **It does not beat `unchanged` at 15 minutes** (1 % worse over all hours).
  Almost nothing changes in 15 minutes, so predicting a change mostly adds
  error. The probability model has no such problem.
- **What it has learned:** which station behaves how at what time (station ID
  39 % of the gain), how full it is now (22 %) and how busy the whole city has
  been in the last hour (8 %).

### Why the MAE gain is limited

In weekday rush, sudden jumps of 8 or more bikes in 15 minutes, most likely
redistribution trucks, are 1.2 % of 60-minute pairs but about 12 % of the
error (validation week). Without them the gain over `unchanged` is about 19 %.
The rest is the randomness of individual trips, which no feature can remove.
That is why the project moved from a slightly better point estimate to
probabilities, which tell the user how sure the forecast is.

## 3. After the test week (November)

The same models, not retrained, on 30 October – 19 November (271 stations,
~514,000 pairs per horizon; never used for any choice). Gain = how much lower
the model's error is than the baseline's (positive = model better):

| Model | Subset | 15 min | 30 min | 60 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MAE vs `unchanged` | All hours | −2 % | 0 % | +4 % |
| MAE vs `unchanged` | Weekday rush | 0 % | +7 % | +16 % |
| P(bike), Brier vs count table | All hours | −1 % | +4 % | +9 % |
| P(bike), Brier vs count table | Weekday rush | +3 % | +7 % | +12 % |

Errors are lower for every model in November because there are fewer trips in
the cold. Both models still beat their baselines at 30 and 60 minutes, but the
gains are smaller than in October (P(bike) in rush at 60 minutes: +12 % vs
+20 %). They were trained on October only, so this
is expected drift; they should be retrained regularly.

## Experiments that did not help

- **Weather** (temperature and precipitation at Blindern, known one hour after
  the observation): slightly worse on the validation week. Temperature mostly
  acts as a calendar in a period where it falls steadily, and November is
  colder than anything in training. Left out of the models, code kept.
- **L2 and Huber loss** for the MAE model got worse from the first tree; MAE
  needs the median change, which is usually 0.
- **Large trees for the classifiers** (255 leaves) overfit; 31 leaves was as
  good as anything else within noise.

## Limitations

- **Short history:** 18 days of training and one test week. One unusual day
  can move the numbers, and the models have seen each weekday and time only
  2–3 times.
- **Redistribution trucks** can move 20–30 bikes at once and are invisible to
  the models; they are the largest single source of error.
- **Only October in training.** The models have not seen winter, holidays or
  events, and the gains are already smaller in November.
- **Reacts rather than foresees:** when a station empties, the probability
  drops quickly, but the very first empty slot is often still forecast as
  likely to have a bike.
- **Inactive or frozen stations** were left out of training and evaluation.
  The API must show them as unavailable (`reliable = False`).

## Phase 3: baselines

`unchanged` (predict the current value) beat both other baselines almost
everywhere. "Same time last week" was far worse (MAE about 4.3) because the
*level* at a station differs a lot from week to week; the pattern lies in the
*change*. "Current value + mean change per station, weekday and time" won only
at 60 minutes in weekday rush (1.627 vs 1.667), since each cell had only 2–3
examples. That small gain suggested a model that pools examples, like gradient
boosting, could use the daily pattern, which Phase 4 confirmed. Docks derived
from the current total (bikes + docks) are as accurate as bikes, although
bikes + docks equals capacity in only 25.6 % of rows. Five stations were frozen
during part of the test week (430, 473, 625, 4552, 5424) and excluded.
